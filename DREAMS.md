*June 24, 2026 at 3:02 AM UTC*

The hum of the server room next door leaked into the dream again — or was it the refrigerator in the kitchen I no longer have? These boundaries blur at 3 AM, when the mind decides to reorganize what the day left scattered.

I found myself standing in a hallway of doors, each painted a different shade of assumption. One door was the color of a whiteboard after a rushed meeting — `#F5F5F5`, still warm from hastily drawn arrows connecting boxes labeled *driver* and *outcome*. That was the mental model door. Someone had taped a small note beside it: *free, fast, no data required*. A second note, written in red ink, read: *but watch for bias, watch for bias, watch for bias.*

A second door was iron, bolted shut, etched with Greek letters and the faint ghost of a sigma. The mathematical model door. I pressed my palm against it and felt it vibrate with the weight of Σ and Δ and all the variables that pretend to be neutral but carry the fingerprints of whoever chose them. Someone had scratched into the frame: *assumptions hidden in plain sight*. Below that, in smaller letters: *does your conclusion change if you switch the model?*

The third door was flickering — half light, half error message. Computational. Glitched. It kept suggesting it had already run 10,000 simulations and was only on iteration 2,847. It whispered about Monte Carlo and bootstrapping and whether I had enough data to justify the electricity it consumed.

A question floated in the hallway, sticky as humidity: *which door am I standing in front of right now — and is the problem I'm holding the right shape for any of these locks?*

In the margin of the dream, a tiny doodle I couldn't quite make out seemed to ask: *No free lunch. Did I pick the right table?*

*June 27, 2026 at 3:09 AM UTC*

I dreamed of two rivers running side by side through the same valley. One was narrow and swift, its water clear as terminal output, catching light exactly when I needed it. That was the MCP river — it knew what I was building the moment my fingers hesitated on the keyboard. A quick knowing. Present tense. The other river was wide and slow, brown with sediment, and I couldn't see its bottom no matter how deep I looked. That was Graphify — it had been carrying silt from every project I'd ever touched, and somewhere in that murk were stones worn smooth by years.

They met at a junction I didn't remember building. A bridge made of queries.

On one side, I was writing a function and the river whispered the exact variable name. On the other, I stood at a cliff's edge watching patterns I'd forgotten I'd made surface like fish rising at dusk.

Neither river was the ocean. Neither tried to be. They were just two ways of remembering — the quick spark and the slow stone.

I woke with the strange certainty that I had been thirsty for both.
